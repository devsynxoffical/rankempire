## Google Business Profile

Your profile is the single biggest lever in local search. Most businesses fill in a third of it.

- Choose the most specific primary category that matches your main revenue service, then add secondary categories for the other services you genuinely offer.
- Add every service you offer with a short, specific description. These feed relevance directly.
- Set the website link to the most relevant landing page, not always the homepage.
- Set your service area to the cities and neighborhoods you actually serve.
- Upload real photos of your team, vehicles, location and work. Twenty to start, five more every month. Stock photos do nothing.
- Post at least weekly: completed jobs, seasonal tips, offers and updates.
- Fill every attribute that applies (accessibility, payment types, amenities).
- Answer common questions in the Q&A section before customers ask them.
- Turn on messaging only if someone will answer within a few hours.

## Consistency across the web

Your business name, address and phone number should match exactly across your website, Google, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and the directories specific to your industry. Inconsistent listings confuse both search engines and customers, and they quietly cap your map rankings.

- Write down one canonical format for the name, suite, phone and hours, and reuse it everywhere.
- Fix the big four first: Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp. Then aggregators and industry directories.
- Remove or correct old listings from previous addresses or phone numbers.
- Add LocalBusiness schema to your site with the same details, and validate it with the free [schema checker](/seo-tools/schema-checker/).

## Reviews

Reviews drive the ranking and the click. The business with 300 recent reviews at 4.8 will usually beat the one with 30 at 5.0, so volume and recency matter as much as the score.

- Ask every happy customer, with a direct link, at the moment of satisfaction.
- Build it into the process: a text after the job, a card with a QR code, a line in the invoice email.
- Respond to every review within a few days, positive or negative. Future customers read the replies.
- Never buy reviews or offer incentives that break platform rules. The penalty is losing all of them.
- Target four to eight new Google reviews a month per location and keep it steady.

## Location and service pages

Create a page for each core service and each important area you serve. Include real details: projects completed nearby, local regulations, travel times, photos and reviews from that area. Avoid copying the same text with the city name swapped, which is the most common reason local pages get filtered out of the index.

- One service, one page. Pages listing ten services rank for none.
- Area pages only where you can say something true and specific about the area.
- Link every service page to the contact page and to the relevant area pages, and link the homepage to all of them.
- Add FAQs pulled from the questions customers actually ask by phone. These also feed [answer engines](/aeo/).

## Technical basics

A slow or broken site caps everything above. The checks that matter for a local business are short:

- The site loads in under three seconds on a phone.
- Every important page is indexed and linked from the navigation or the homepage.
- Name, address, phone and hours on the site match the profile exactly.
- The contact form works on a phone and sends to someone who answers.
- There is one version of the site (www or non-www, https) and the rest redirect to it.

## Local links

Sponsor local teams and events, join your chamber of commerce, partner with complementary businesses, get listed on supplier and manufacturer locator pages, and pitch local news stories. Local links are a strong prominence signal that most competitors never build.

- Aim for two to four genuinely local links a month.
- Prioritize sites that are clearly about your city or your industry over generic directories.
- Avoid paid link packages. They are the fastest way to lose what you have built.

## FAQ

### What matters most for ranking in the Google map pack?

Relevance (a complete profile and site that clearly match the search), distance (which you cannot change) and prominence (reviews, links and mentions). The profile and reviews are the fastest levers; links and mentions compound over months.

### How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

Weekly is enough to keep the profile active. Posts are a minor ranking factor on their own, but profiles with recent activity hold positions better and convert more viewers into calls.

### Do citations still matter for local SEO?

Consistent citations matter; volume does not. Fix the major platforms and your industry's directories, make every listing identical, and stop there. Hundreds of low-quality directory listings add nothing.
