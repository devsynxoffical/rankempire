SEO for contractors comes down to three things: winning the Google Maps pack in the towns you serve, having one strong page for every job you want more of, and earning reviews that mention the job and the town. This guide covers how plumbers, electricians, roofers, HVAC companies, painters and other home service contractors rank in Google and in AI answers, and links to the detailed playbook for each trade.

## Why contractor SEO is different

Most contractor searches are local and urgent. "Water heater repair near me", "roof leak repair Katy" or "electrician open now" are decided in minutes, usually from the map pack on a phone. That makes contractor SEO closer to [local SEO](/local-seo/) than to national content marketing. The website still matters, because Google reads it to decide which jobs you do and where, but the Google Business Profile, reviews and service area signals decide most of the calls.

AI assistants now add a second path. ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews answer questions like "best roofer in Plano for hail damage" by reading the same profiles, reviews and service pages. Contractors with clear, specific information get named; vague ones get skipped, as we explain in [AEO vs GEO vs SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-geo-vs-seo/).

## 1. Set up the Google Business Profile for the job, not the company

Choose the most specific primary category, such as Plumber, Electrician, Roofing Contractor or HVAC Contractor, then add secondary categories only for work you actually do. List every service with a short description, set realistic service areas, keep hours accurate (including emergency hours) and upload real job photos every month. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist/) walks through every field.

For starter keyword lists by trade, see our guide to [SEO keywords by industry](/blog/seo-keywords-by-industry/).

## 2. Build one page per high-value job

A single "Services" page cannot rank for water heaters, sewer lines, panel upgrades and roof replacements at the same time. Each job you want more of needs its own page with:

- What the job involves and how long it takes
- Price ranges or starting prices, with what changes the price
- Brands, materials or equipment you work with
- Photos from real projects
- Licensing, insurance and warranty details
- A short FAQ using the questions customers ask on the phone

Starting prices matter more than most contractors expect. Customers and AI assistants both compare, and a page that says "water heater replacement from $1,450 installed" is easier to recommend than "call for a quote".

## 3. Add service area pages only where you really work

Pages for each town you serve can rank well, but only with real local detail: jobs completed there, local permit or code notes, reviews from customers in that town and the services offered there. Pages that only swap the city name rarely rank and can drag down the whole site.

## 4. Turn reviews into ranking signals

Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking factors you control. Ask every customer, by text, within a day of the job, and ask them to mention the job and the town: "they replaced our water heater in Sugar Land the same day" helps far more than "great service". Reply to every review, good or bad.

## 5. Use the right structured data

Mark up the site with LocalBusiness data using the most specific type schema.org offers, such as Plumber, Electrician, HVACBusiness, RoofingContractor or GeneralContractor, plus Service markup for each job page and FAQ markup where the questions are unique. Check it with our free [schema checker](/seo-tools/schema-checker/).

## 6. Earn local links

The best links for contractors are local and real: suppliers and manufacturers' dealer locators, trade associations, the chamber of commerce, local news coverage of a project, sponsorships of youth teams and partner businesses such as realtors and property managers. A handful of these beats hundreds of directory links.

## 7. Plan for seasons

Contractor demand is seasonal: AC repair in early summer, furnaces in fall, roofing after storms, pools in spring. Publish and update seasonal pages two to three months before demand peaks, so they are indexed and ranking when customers start searching.

## FAQ

### What is the most important SEO factor for contractors?

For most contractors it is the Google Business Profile: the right primary category, accurate service areas, steady reviews that mention the job and town, and regular photos. The website supports it with one strong page per job.

### Should contractors publish prices on their website?

Publishing starting prices or ranges usually helps. Customers and AI assistants compare options, and specific numbers make a contractor easier to choose. You can explain what changes the final price without committing to a fixed quote.

### Do contractors need a page for every city they serve?

Only for towns where you have real work, reviews and something specific to say. A few strong service area pages outperform dozens of near-identical ones.

### Is SEO or Google Local Services Ads better for contractors?

They work together. Local Services Ads buy immediate visibility for a fee per lead, while SEO builds rankings that keep producing calls without paying per click. Many contractors use ads while their organic rankings grow.
