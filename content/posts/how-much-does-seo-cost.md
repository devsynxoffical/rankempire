## What drives the price

The price of SEO is mostly the price of skilled hours. What changes the number of hours is:

- **Competition.** Ranking a locksmith in a town of 40,000 takes a fraction of the work of ranking a personal injury firm in Los Angeles. Competitive markets need more content, more links and more time.
- **Scope.** One location and five services is a small program. Twelve locations, a product catalog or multiple countries multiplies the pages that need work.
- **Starting point.** A site with technical problems, thin pages or a penalty history needs cleanup before growth work can begin. The [SEO audit](/seo-audit/) is where that gets priced.
- **Goals and speed.** Wanting to lead the market in six months costs more than wanting steady growth over two years, because the work gets front-loaded.
- **Who does the work.** Freelancers, small agencies, large agencies and in-house hires all carry different overheads, and the quality range inside each group is wide.

## Why cheap SEO usually costs more

Plans under $500 a month exist, and some are honest about being limited. The problem is the ones that are not. At that price the work has to be automated or outsourced at very low rates, which in practice means spun content, directory spam, purchased links and reports that count things that do not matter. The cleanup afterward, which we are asked to do often, costs more than a proper program would have.

A useful rule: if a quote promises specific rankings, guaranteed first-page results, hundreds of links a month or results in 30 days, the price is irrelevant because the method is going to hurt you. ## How to compare SEO quotes

Ask every agency the same seven questions and compare the answers rather than the prices:

1. **What exactly is delivered each month?** Pages, content pieces, links, technical tickets, profile updates. Vague "optimization" is not a deliverable.
2. **Who does the work and who do I talk to?** A named strategist who does the work beats an account manager relaying messages.
3. **How do you earn links?** Outreach, PR and content are good answers. Networks, packages and "our partner sites" are not.
4. **What does the first 90 days look like?** A credible agency can describe the sequence: audit, fixes, foundations, then growth. See our [90-day local SEO roadmap](/blog/local-seo-90-day-roadmap/) for an example.
5. **How do you report?** Rankings and traffic are fine, but calls, leads and revenue from organic should be in the report.
6. **What is the contract?** Month to month after onboarding is fair. Twelve-month lock-ins with no exit protect the agency, not you.
7. **Can I see real results?** Screenshots of Search Console from real clients, with permission, as on our [case studies page](/case-studies/), rather than mockups.

## Is SEO worth the cost for your business?

Run the arithmetic before you sign anything. You need three numbers: the value of a customer, your close rate from leads, and a realistic estimate of extra leads from search.

Take a plumbing company where an average job is worth $450, one in three leads becomes a job, and a solid local program could add 25 leads a month within six months. That is about 8 extra jobs, or $3,600 a month, against a $1,200 plan. The return is clearly positive, and it grows, because rankings compound while the fee stays flat.

Now take a hobby store with a $30 average order and no online sales. Even a strong program might not pay back. SEO is not the right channel for every business, and an honest agency will tell you that in the first call.

The time horizon matters too. SEO rarely pays back inside the first three months, because the early work is foundations. The verified reports on our results page show the same curve on every campaign: impressions move first, then positions, then clicks and leads, usually bending between months three and six.

## FAQ

### How much does SEO cost per month for a small business?

Most small businesses pay between $500 and $2,500 a month. Single-location local service businesses are at the lower end; businesses in competitive metros or with several locations are at the upper end. RankEmpire's local plans start at $599 a month.

### Is it cheaper to do SEO yourself?

In cash, yes. In time, usually not. A disciplined owner can handle the basics in about five hours a week using guides like our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist/) and the free tools on this site. Agencies make sense when that time is worth more spent on the business, or when the market is competitive enough to need specialist work.

### Why do agencies not publish prices?

Because scope varies, and because some prefer to anchor to your budget. We publish ours because a clear price is easier to compare and keeps the conversation about the work rather than the negotiation.

### How long do I have to commit?

Avoid long lock-ins. A three-month onboarding period is reasonable because the early work is front-loaded; after that, month to month with 30 days notice is fair. That is how RankEmpire plans work.

### Does a higher price mean better results?

Not by itself. Price reflects hours and overhead. What predicts results is the method, the people doing the work and the fit between the plan and your market. Use the seven questions above to compare, not the fee.

### What results should I expect for the money?

Early movement on branded and low-competition terms within four to eight weeks, meaningful ranking gains for core terms in four to six months, and lead growth that compounds from there. Any agency promising specific positions is guessing or cheating.
