Choosing an SEO company is hard because most of the work is invisible until months later. Two agencies can send near-identical proposals at very different prices, and the difference only shows up in month six, when one has built rankings that compound and the other has a report full of activity and a flat graph. This guide gives you the twelve questions that separate them, the red flags to walk away from, and a simple way to compare proposals side by side, whether you are hiring an [SEO company in New York](/locations/new-york/), a [Chicago SEO agency](/locations/chicago/) or a specialist for your industry.

## Start with what you need, not who is selling

Before talking to any agency, write down three things. They make every conversation sharper and every proposal easier to compare.

- **The outcome.** More calls from the map pack, more online sales, more demo requests, or more qualified leads from a specific city. "More traffic" is not an outcome.
- **The scope.** One location or many, one service or twenty, a local market or the whole country.
- **The budget range and timeline.** A realistic monthly figure and how long you can commit before judging results. Our guide on [how much SEO costs](/blog/how-much-does-seo-cost/) gives ranges by business type.

With those three written down, you can tell quickly whether an agency is proposing to solve your problem or selling its standard package.

## The 12 questions to ask every SEO company

### 1. What will you do in the first 90 days, in order?

A credible agency describes a sequence: audit, technical fixes, foundations, then content and authority. If the answer starts with "we will write four blog posts a month", the order is wrong. Our [90-day local SEO roadmap](/blog/local-seo-90-day-roadmap/) is an example of what a real sequence looks like.

### 2. What exactly is delivered each month?

Ask for deliverables you can count: pages built or rewritten, technical issues fixed, links earned, profile updates, reports. "Ongoing optimization" is not a deliverable.

### 3. Who does the work, and who do I talk to?

Find out whether a strategist does the work or an account manager relays messages to an outsourced team. At RankEmpire every account has one senior strategist who does the work and takes the calls, led by founder Gaurav Kapoor; ask any agency you consider to name the person.

### 4. How do you earn links?

Good answers: outreach, digital PR, partnerships, original data, content worth citing. Bad answers: "our network", "guest post packages", "100 links a month". Link schemes are the fastest way to lose rankings, as covered in [why most SEO campaigns fail](/blog/why-most-seo-campaigns-fail/).

### 5. Can I see real results?

Ask for Search Console screenshots from real clients, shared with permission, not rank tracker charts or mockups. Clicks and impressions from Google's own data are hard to fake. Our [case studies](/case-studies/) are all real Search Console reports for this reason.

### 6. How will you report, and what will you measure?

Rankings and traffic are fine as leading indicators, but the report should connect to calls, forms, bookings and revenue. Ask to see a sample report.

### 7. What is the contract?

Month to month after a short onboarding period is fair. A twelve-month lock-in with no exit clause protects the agency, not you.

### 8. Who owns the work if we stop?

You should own every page, piece of content, account and profile created for you. Some agencies keep content on their own domains or hold your Google Business Profile hostage. Get ownership in writing.

### 9. What do you need from us?

Good SEO needs access (site, Search Console, analytics, Business Profile) and some of your time for approvals and expertise. An agency that asks for nothing will produce generic work.

### 10. How do you handle AI search?

Search now includes featured snippets, AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity answers, and AI agents that compare and book. Ask how the agency covers [answer engine](/aeo/), [generative engine](/geo/) and [AI agent optimization](/blog/ai-agent-optimization/), not just the classic results.

### 11. Have you worked in my industry or market?

Industry experience shortens the learning curve: an agency that has run [SEO for electricians](/seo-for-electricians/) knows emergency searches and permit content; one that has done [auto detailing SEO](/seo-for-auto-detailing/) knows that model-level and package pages drive bookings. Ask for the specific playbook, not a logo slide.

### 12. What will you not do?

Good agencies turn down work that will not pay back and tell you when SEO is the wrong channel. If every answer is yes, be careful.

## Red flags that mean walk away

- **Guaranteed rankings.** Nobody controls Google. A guarantee of position one means either a meaningless keyword or a risky tactic.
- **Results in 30 days.** Real SEO takes three to six months to compound. Fast promises usually mean paid links or spam.
- **Secret methods.** "Proprietary techniques we cannot share" usually hide something you would not approve.
- **Huge link counts.** Hundreds of links a month at a low price are almost always low quality.
- **No access for you.** If you cannot see your own Search Console, analytics or Business Profile, you cannot verify anything.
- **Long lock-ins.** Twelve or twenty-four months with no exit is a sign the agency does not expect you to stay voluntarily.
- **Reports with only rankings.** Positions for obscure keywords are easy to show and do not pay bills.

## How to compare proposals side by side

Put each proposal into the same five rows and the right choice usually becomes obvious:

| Compare | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| First 90 days | A clear sequence starting with an audit and technical fixes |
| Monthly deliverables | Countable outputs: pages, fixes, links, profile work |
| People | A named strategist who does the work |
| Measurement | Calls, leads and revenue next to rankings and traffic |
| Terms | Month to month after onboarding, you own everything |

Price comes last. A cheaper proposal that fails three rows costs more in the end than a fair one that passes all five.

## Local agency or remote specialist?

A local agency knows your city's neighborhoods and competition, and some businesses value meeting in person. A remote specialist may know your industry better and often costs less because overheads are lower. What matters more than location is whether the team understands your market. For local businesses, ask any agency, local or not, to show how they would rank you across your whole service area, not just near your office; that is what a rank grid report is for. ## When SEO consulting is the better choice

If you have an in-house marketer or developer, you may not need a full-service agency. [SEO consulting](/seo-consulting/) gives your team the strategy, audits and reviews while they do the work, usually at a lower monthly cost. It works best when someone internal has the time and the access to ship changes.

## FAQ

### How do I know if an SEO company is legitimate?

Check for real Search Console results shared with permission, a named strategist, clear monthly deliverables, transparent link building methods and fair contract terms. Avoid any company that guarantees rankings or results in 30 days.

### How much should I pay an SEO company?

Most small businesses pay $500 to $2,500 a month, multi-location and e-commerce programs $2,000 to $8,000, and national or enterprise programs more. The right price funds the full sequence of work rather than a piece of it. See our [pricing](/seo-packages/) for exact plans.

### How long before an SEO company shows results?

Expect early movement on low-competition and branded searches within four to eight weeks, meaningful gains for core terms in four to six months, and compounding growth after that. Leading indicators like impressions and positions should move first.

### Should I sign a long-term SEO contract?

A short onboarding period of around three months is reasonable because early work is front-loaded. After that, month to month with 30 days notice is fair and keeps the agency accountable.

### What should an SEO company report every month?

Work completed, rankings and visibility for target searches, organic traffic, and the business results that matter: calls, form submissions, bookings and revenue from organic search, plus what comes next.

### Is it better to hire an SEO company or a freelancer?

Freelancers can be excellent for focused tasks and smaller budgets. Agencies make sense when you need several skills at once, such as technical SEO, content, links and local work, or continuity if one person is unavailable.
