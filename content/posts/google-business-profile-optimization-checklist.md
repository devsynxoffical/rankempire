Your Google Business Profile is the most important page you do not host. For most local businesses it produces more calls than the website does, and it is the single biggest factor in whether you appear in the map pack. Most profiles we audit are about a third complete. This checklist is the full list, in the order we work through it for clients, with the reason behind each item so you can prioritize. It is the same process behind our [Google Business Profile SEO service](/google-business-profile-seo/).

## Before you start: ownership and verification

- Confirm you own the profile. Search your business name in Google Maps, click the listing and check for "You manage this Business Profile". If an old employee or agency owns it, request access through the listing before doing anything else.
- Verify it. Unverified profiles have limited features and lower trust. Video verification is the most common method now; have your signage, vehicle and a workspace ready to show.
- Remove duplicates. Search for your phone number and address in Maps. Duplicate listings split reviews and confuse Google. Mark duplicates as "permanently closed" or request a merge through support.
- Check for suspensions. A suspended profile shows a notice in the dashboard. Fix the cause (usually a name, address or category violation) before appealing.

## Business information

- **Business name.** Use your real-world name exactly, with no added keywords or city names. "Joe's Plumbing" is correct; "Joe's Plumbing Houston Emergency Plumber" gets suspended.
- **Primary category.** This is the strongest relevance signal on the profile. Pick the most specific category that describes your main revenue service. "Emergency plumber" or "Plumber", not "Contractor". Check what the top three competitors in the map pack use.
- **Secondary categories.** Add every category that genuinely describes a service you sell. Do not add categories for services you do not offer.
- **Address or service area.** Storefronts show the address. Service area businesses (plumbers, cleaners, movers) hide the address and set the cities and zip codes they actually serve. Keep the service area realistic; a 200-mile radius from one van reduces trust.
- **Phone number.** Use a local number as the primary. If you use call tracking, set the tracking number as primary and add your real number as an additional number, so your name, address and phone stay consistent across the web.
- **Website link.** Point it at the most relevant page, which is usually the homepage for single-service businesses and the location page for multi-location businesses.
- **Hours.** Set regular hours and keep them accurate, add special hours for holidays, and set "more hours" (drive-through, pickup, senior hours) where they apply. Wrong hours generate negative reviews.
- **Opening date.** Add it; longevity is a trust signal and it is a required field for some features.

## Services, products and attributes

- **Services.** Add every service with a description of one to two sentences and, where you can, a price or price range. Services feed relevance for searches like "water heater replacement near me" directly. Use Google's suggested services for your category first, then add custom ones.
- **Products.** Retailers and restaurants should add products with photos and prices. Service businesses can use the product section for packages ("Spring HVAC tune-up, $129").
- **Attributes.** Fill every attribute that applies: accessibility, payment types, amenities, "women-owned", "veteran-owned", "online appointments" and so on. Attributes appear as filters and as badges in results.
- **Business description.** Up to 750 characters. Lead with what you do and where, in plain language, then what makes you different. Do not stuff keywords; it does not help ranking and it reads badly.

## Photos and video

- Upload at least 20 real photos to start: exterior from the street, interior, team, vehicles, equipment and completed work. Profiles with more photos get more direction requests and calls.
- Add five or more new photos every month. Recency matters.
- Name files descriptively before uploading ("tankless-water-heater-install-katy-tx.jpg") and shoot in good light. Skip stock photos; customers and Google both notice.
- Add a logo and a cover photo that shows the business, not a graphic.
- Add short videos (under 30 seconds) of the team at work. They stand out in the photo carousel.

## Reviews

- Set up a short review link (from the "Ask for reviews" button in the dashboard) and put it everywhere: text message after the job, invoice email, a QR code on a card.
- Ask every customer at the moment of satisfaction. A system that produces four to eight reviews a month beats a one-time push.
- Reply to every review within two days. Thank positive reviewers by name and mention the service. For negative reviews, stay calm, state the facts briefly and take it offline. Future customers read your replies more than the review.
- Never incentivize reviews or gate them (only asking happy customers through a filter). Both violate Google's policies and can wipe out your reviews.
- Report fake reviews through the dashboard, with evidence. It takes time, but it works.

## Posts, Q&A and messaging

- **Posts.** Publish one post a week: a completed project, a seasonal tip, an offer or an event. Posts keep the profile active and give you a place to put calls to action.
- **Q&A.** Anyone can ask and answer questions on your profile, so pre-fill it. Post the ten questions customers ask by phone and answer them from your business account. Monitor for new questions weekly.
- **Messaging.** Turn it on only if someone will respond within a few hours. Slow responses get the feature disabled and look bad.
- **Booking.** If you take appointments, connect a booking provider or add your booking link so customers can book from the profile.

## Consistency across the web

Your name, address and phone must match exactly between the profile, your website, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and your industry directories. Inconsistency erodes Google's confidence in your details.

- Write down one canonical format for the name, suite number, phone and hours.
- Fix the major platforms first, then industry directories, then data aggregators.
- Add LocalBusiness schema to your website with the same details and validate it with our free [schema checker](/seo-tools/schema-checker/).

## Multi-location businesses

- One profile per physical location, each with its own phone, hours, photos and reviews.
- A unique location page on the website for each profile, linked from the profile's website field, with real local detail rather than city-swapped text.
- Use the dashboard's location groups so staff can manage profiles without owner access.
- Keep categories consistent across locations unless the services genuinely differ.

## Track what matters

The profile's own insights show how people found you (discovery versus direct), what they did (calls, direction requests, website clicks) and which searches triggered the listing. Watch three things monthly:

- Calls and direction requests, which are the leads the profile produces.
- The search terms that show your profile. New terms mean growing relevance.
- Your map pack position across your service area, measured on a rank grid rather than from your office, as explained in our [90-day local SEO roadmap](/blog/local-seo-90-day-roadmap/).

## The checklist in order

1. Claim, verify and clean up duplicates.
2. Set the real name, the most specific primary category and accurate address or service area.
3. Complete services with descriptions, products, attributes and the description.
4. Upload 20 photos and keep adding monthly.
5. Build the review system and reply to everything.
6. Post weekly, pre-fill Q&A, enable messaging only if you can answer.
7. Make name, address and phone identical everywhere, with schema on the site.
8. Track calls, searches and grid position monthly.

## FAQ

### How long does it take for Google Business Profile changes to affect rankings?

Category and service changes usually show within a few days to two weeks. Reviews, photos and posts compound over months. Profiles that were barely filled in often see map pack movement within the first month of a complete setup.

### Can I add keywords to my business name?

No. Google's guidelines require the real-world name, and keyword-stuffed names are the most common reason for suspensions. Put the keywords in categories, services and the description instead.

### How many reviews do I need to rank in the map pack?

Enough to be competitive with the businesses currently showing for your searches, and a steady flow afterward. For most single-location service businesses that is four to eight new reviews a month, every month.

### Do Google posts help ranking?

Marginally on their own, but active profiles hold positions better and convert more viewers, and posts are a free place to promote offers. One post a week is the right cadence.

### Should a service area business show its address?

Only if customers visit the location. If they do not, hide the address and set the service area. Showing a home address for a service business is against the guidelines and can get the profile suspended.
