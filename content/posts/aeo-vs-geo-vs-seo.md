Search is no longer one results page. The same question now gets answered in a Google AI Overview, a ChatGPT reply, a Perplexity answer with footnotes, a voice assistant and, still, ten blue links. Each surface picks its sources a little differently, and three acronyms have grown up around that: SEO, AEO and GEO.

This guide explains what each one actually changes, how AI answers choose what to cite, and the 12 steps we run for clients who want to be the source, not the footnote that never gets clicked. Everything here is what we do inside the [AEO](/aeo/) and [GEO](/geo/) programs at RankEmpire, with the parts that overlap with classic [search engine optimization](/seo-services/) called out, because most of the work is shared.

## The three terms in one table

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Search engine optimization | Answer engine optimization | Generative engine optimization |
| Where you show up | Organic results, map pack, image and video results | Featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice answers, AI Overview citations | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and Copilot answers; AI Overviews and AI Mode |

The important point hides in the second row. SEO, AEO and GEO are not three websites or three teams. They are three ways the same page can be consumed. A service page that ranks well, answers its questions in tight passages and is mentioned across the web will perform on all three surfaces. A page that only does one of those will leak demand to a competitor who does all three.

## What search engine optimization still decides

Nothing about AI search makes classic SEO optional, for a plain reason: the AI systems retrieve their candidate sources from a search index first. Google's AI Overviews are built on top of Google's ranking systems. Perplexity and ChatGPT search run live web queries and read the top results before writing. If your page cannot be crawled, is not indexed, or sits on page four, it is rarely in the candidate set at all.

So the foundation is unchanged:

- Technical health: crawlable, indexable, fast, mobile-first. Our [technical SEO](/technical-seo/) work starts every program because it gates everything else.
- Relevance: one page per intent, with a clear job, built from real [keyword research](/keyword-research/) rather than guesses.
- Authority: links and mentions from sites that matter in your market, earned through [link building](/link-building/) and digital PR.
- Experience: pages that people actually use, measured in clicks, calls and conversions.

## What answer engine optimization adds

Answer engines take a page and extract a passage. The featured snippet at the top of Google, the People Also Ask accordions, voice assistants reading one sentence aloud, and the citations under an AI Overview all work at the passage level. AEO is the discipline of making your best answer easy to lift.

Three habits make the difference:

1. **Lead with the answer.** Put a direct, complete answer in the first sentence or two under each heading, then expand. Search engines quote the first clean definition they find, not the one buried after three paragraphs of context.
2. **Make headings questions people actually ask.** Google's own People Also Ask boxes and your Search Console query report tell you the exact wording. A heading that mirrors the question is far more likely to be matched to it.
3. **Mark it up.** FAQPage, HowTo, Product, Service, Organization and Person schema label what each block is. Validate it with our free [schema checker](/seo-tools/schema-checker/) rather than assuming it parses.

AEO is also where zero-click search lives. A large share of informational queries now end on the results page. That is not a loss if the snippet carries your brand name and the next step for the customer is to search for you directly, which is exactly what we see in branded query growth for clients who own the top answers in their category.

## What generative engine optimization changes

Generative engines do something different: they synthesize. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Mode read several sources, combine them and write an original answer with citations. To be cited you need to be retrieved, trusted and specific. That last word matters most. A generic page that says "we offer quality service at competitive prices" gives a language model nothing to use. A page that says "a two-location dental practice should expect 4 to 6 reviews a month per location to keep pace with the map pack in a mid-sized metro" is a claim a model can quote and attribute.

GEO work therefore concentrates on five things:

- **Entity clarity.** The engines need to know who you are. A consistent name, address and description across your site, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, industry directories and press, plus Organization and Person schema that point to those profiles, builds the entity graph the models lean on.
- **Original, quotable information.** First-party data, benchmarks, named processes, prices and timelines. Everything we publish in our [case studies](/case-studies/) is real Search Console data for this reason: real numbers get quoted, vague claims do not.
- **Mentions across the web.** Models weigh how often a brand appears alongside a topic in the corpus they were trained on and in the pages they retrieve. Brand mentions on relevant sites, even unlinked, raise the odds of being named. This is a shift from classic link building, where only the link counted.
- **Freshness.** Retrieval-based engines prefer recently updated sources for anything time-sensitive. Dated, maintained pages with visible update timestamps win over stale ones.
- **Access for AI crawlers.** GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended all respect robots.txt. If you block them, you cannot be cited. Our robots file allows them on purpose, and we check that with the free [robots.txt checker](/seo-tools/robots-txt-checker/).

## How AI answers actually pick sources

Nobody outside the labs has the full recipe, but the behavior is observable, and after tracking citations across hundreds of prompts for clients the patterns are consistent enough to act on.

:::kt
Top 10|Most AI citations come from pages that already rank on page one for a closely related query
3 to 5|Typical number of sources cited per generative answer, so the bar is higher than ranking tenth
Passage|Engines cite the specific passage that answered the sub-question, not the page as a whole
:::

What we see repeatedly:

- Citations cluster on pages with clear structure: short paragraphs, descriptive H2 and H3 headings, lists and tables that make a fact easy to extract.
- Pages with a named author, an about page and visible credentials get cited more often than anonymous content on the same topic. Experience and trust are evaluated, not assumed.
- Comparison and "best" queries pull from third-party sources far more than from the brands themselves. If you want to be named in "best SEO agency for dentists", you need to be on the lists other sites publish, not only on your own site.
- Local prompts, such as "good HVAC company near Phoenix", lean heavily on Google Business Profile data, reviews and the same signals that drive the map pack. [Local SEO](/local-seo/) and [Google Business Profile optimization](/google-business-profile-seo/) are GEO for local businesses.
- Numbers get quoted. A page with a benchmark, a price range or a timeline is cited where a page of adjectives is skipped.

## The overlap, and why you should not run three separate programs

If you draw the three disciplines as circles, around 70 percent of the work sits in the overlap. Technical health, clear information architecture, a page per intent, well-structured content, schema, authority and a visible, consistent brand entity serve all three. The remaining 30 percent is surface-specific: snippet formatting for AEO, citation tracking and mention building for GEO, map pack work for local.

That is why we run them as one program with one roadmap rather than selling AEO and GEO as add-ons. The practical sequence is below.

## The 12-step playbook

1. **Audit crawlability and indexation first.** Confirm every important page is indexed, nothing valuable is blocked, and AI crawlers are allowed. Start with a [free SEO audit](/seo-audit/) if you have never had one.
2. **Map every query to one page.** One intent, one page. Merge cannibalizing pages and split overloaded ones.
3. **Rewrite openings to answer first.** The first 40 to 60 words under each heading should answer the heading's question completely.
4. **Turn headings into real questions.** Pull wording from Search Console, People Also Ask and your sales calls.
5. **Add facts a model can quote.** Prices, timelines, benchmarks, named steps, original data. Replace adjectives with specifics.
6. **Add and validate schema.** Organization with sameAs profiles, Person for authors, Service, FAQPage where the FAQ is unique, Article with dates.
7. **Put a real author on everything.** Name, role, photo, bio page, outside profiles. Link author pages from every article.
8. **Build the entity footprint.** Identical business details on Google, Bing, Apple, LinkedIn, Crunchbase and your industry directories.
9. **Earn mentions, not only links.** Guest contributions, podcasts, roundups, expert quotes and listicles in your category. Measure brand mentions alongside backlinks.
10. **Keep pages fresh.** Visible updated dates, a quarterly review of your top 20 pages, and a changelog of what changed.
11. **Track AI visibility.** Run a fixed set of prompts monthly across AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini and log who gets cited. Our monthly reports include share of voice across these engines next to rankings.
12. **Report revenue, not just rankings.** AI referral traffic converts well because the intent is already qualified. Tag it and track it to calls and sales.

## A worked example: one service page, three surfaces

Take a page about emergency plumbing in Houston. For classic search it needs the basics: title, H1, service details, reviews, internal links from the Houston location page and the [plumbers industry page](/seo-for-plumbers/), and a few local citations. For answer engines it needs a short passage that says exactly what a 24-hour callout costs and how fast a plumber arrives, under a heading that asks that question. For generative engines it needs the business to appear on lists of Houston plumbers, a profile with consistent details, reviews that mention emergency work, and a page that was updated this quarter rather than three years ago.

Same page. One roadmap. Three ways of being found.

## How to measure progress

Set a baseline before you change anything, then track monthly:

- Classic: rankings for your 20 primary keywords, organic clicks, calls and leads from organic.
- Answer engines: which snippets and People Also Ask results you own, impressions on queries where you have no clicks (a sign you are the zero-click answer).
- Generative engines: a prompt set of 30 to 50 questions your customers ask, run across the main assistants, scored by whether you are named, cited or linked. Share of voice against three competitors.
- Entity: brand search volume, brand mentions, referring domains.

If the generative numbers lag while classic rankings are strong, the usual causes are missing specifics, no named author, or thin presence on third-party lists. If classic rankings lag, go back to the foundation.

## FAQ

### Is AEO or GEO replacing SEO?

No. Both depend on the same search indexes and ranking systems to find candidate sources. SEO gets you into the candidate set, AEO makes your passage the one that gets quoted, and GEO makes your brand the one that gets named. Treat them as layers on one program.

### How long does it take to get cited in AI Overviews or ChatGPT?

For pages that already rank on page one, structural changes often show up in citations within four to eight weeks. For new topics, expect three to six months, the same horizon as classic SEO, because you first need to rank and build the entity signals.

### Should I block AI crawlers to protect my content?

If you want to be cited, no. Blocking GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot or Google-Extended removes you from those answers. Publishers with content they license may choose to block; service businesses almost always benefit from being included.

### Does FAQ schema still help?

Google has reduced FAQ rich results for most commercial sites, so do not expect the dropdowns in results. The structure still helps answer and generative engines parse your questions and answers, and it costs nothing when the FAQ is genuinely unique to the page.

### Can a small local business do GEO?

Yes, and it is often easier than for national brands because the competition in a metro is thinner. A complete Google Business Profile, consistent citations, steady reviews, a page per service with specific details and a few local mentions will get a local business named in AI answers for its category and city.
