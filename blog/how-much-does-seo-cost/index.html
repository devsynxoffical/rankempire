How Much Does SEO Cost in 2026? Real Prices, What You Get and How to Compare

"How much does SEO cost?" is the first question on almost every call we take, and the honest answer is a range with reasons behind it. This guide gives you the ranges we see in the US market in 2026, what each price level actually buys, the questions that separate a fair quote from a bad one, and how to judge whether SEO is worth it for your business at all. Where we quote RankEmpire's own numbers, they are the ones on our SEO packages page, so you can check them.

The short answer

$500 to $1,500 / monthLocal SEO for a single-location service business, run by a small agency or specialist
$1,500 to $5,000 / monthMulti-location local, regional, small e-commerce or SaaS programs with content and links
$5,000 to $20,000+ / monthNational, enterprise and competitive e-commerce or SaaS programs with dedicated teams

Three other price shapes exist alongside monthly retainers: one-time audits ($500 to $5,000), hourly SEO consulting ($100 to $300 per hour) and project pricing for a migration or a site rebuild ($3,000 to $30,000). Most businesses end up on a monthly plan, because SEO is ongoing work and the results compound.

What drives the price

The price of SEO is mostly the price of skilled hours. What changes the number of hours is:

  • Competition. Ranking a locksmith in a town of 40,000 takes a fraction of the work of ranking a personal injury firm in Los Angeles. Competitive markets need more content, more links and more time.
  • Scope. One location and five services is a small program. Twelve locations, a product catalog or multiple countries multiplies the pages that need work.
  • Starting point. A site with technical problems, thin pages or a penalty history needs cleanup before growth work can begin. The SEO audit is where that gets priced.
  • Goals and speed. Wanting to lead the market in six months costs more than wanting steady growth over two years, because the work gets front-loaded.
  • Who does the work. Freelancers, small agencies, large agencies and in-house hires all carry different overheads, and the quality range inside each group is wide.

Why cheap SEO usually costs more

Plans under $500 a month exist, and some are honest about being limited. The problem is the ones that are not. At that price the work has to be automated or outsourced at very low rates, which in practice means spun content, directory spam, purchased links and reports that count things that do not matter. The cleanup afterward, which we are asked to do often, costs more than a proper program would have.

A useful rule: if a quote promises specific rankings, guaranteed first-page results, hundreds of links a month or results in 30 days, the price is irrelevant because the method is going to hurt you. We wrote about the common failure patterns in why most SEO campaigns fail.

How to compare SEO quotes

Ask every agency the same seven questions and compare the answers rather than the prices:

  1. What exactly is delivered each month? Pages, content pieces, links, technical tickets, profile updates. Vague "optimization" is not a deliverable.
  2. Who does the work and who do I talk to? A named strategist who does the work beats an account manager relaying messages.
  3. How do you earn links? Outreach, PR and content are good answers. Networks, packages and "our partner sites" are not.
  4. What does the first 90 days look like? A credible agency can describe the sequence: audit, fixes, foundations, then growth. See our 90-day local SEO roadmap for an example.
  5. How do you report? Rankings and traffic are fine, but calls, leads and revenue from organic should be in the report.
  6. What is the contract? Month to month after onboarding is fair. Twelve-month lock-ins with no exit protect the agency, not you.
  7. Can I see real results? Screenshots of Search Console from real clients, with permission, as on our case studies page, rather than mockups.

Is SEO worth the cost for your business?

Run the arithmetic before you sign anything. You need three numbers: the value of a customer, your close rate from leads, and a realistic estimate of extra leads from search.

Take a plumbing company where an average job is worth $450, one in three leads becomes a job, and a solid local program could add 25 leads a month within six months. That is about 8 extra jobs, or $3,600 a month, against a $1,200 plan. The return is clearly positive, and it grows, because rankings compound while the fee stays flat.

Now take a hobby store with a $30 average order and no online sales. Even a strong program might not pay back. SEO is not the right channel for every business, and an honest agency will tell you that in the first call.

The time horizon matters too. SEO rarely pays back inside the first three months, because the early work is foundations. The verified reports on our results page show the same curve on every campaign: impressions move first, then positions, then clicks and leads, usually bending between months three and six.

SEO cost by business type

  • Local service businesses (contractors, clinics, salons): $600 to $2,500 a month. The map pack decides most leads, so the profile, reviews and area pages are the bulk of the work. See the industry playbooks, for example SEO for HVAC companies or SEO for dentists.
  • Multi-location businesses: $2,000 to $8,000 a month, scaling with locations, since each needs its own profile, page and review flow.
  • E-commerce stores: $2,000 to $15,000 a month depending on catalog size and competition. Category pages, technical control of faceted navigation and content drive the cost. See e-commerce SEO.
  • SaaS and B2B: $3,000 to $15,000 a month. Fewer, higher-value keywords, comparison and integration pages, and attribution to pipeline. See SaaS SEO.
  • Luxury, travel and event businesses (charters, limousines, exotic rentals, venues): $1,500 to $6,000 a month, because fewer searches carry much higher values and route, fleet and venue pages do most of the work. See SEO for private jet charter companies, yacht charter companies and wedding venues and event companies.
  • National and enterprise brands: $8,000 to $30,000+ a month, with dedicated teams and governance. See enterprise SEO.

One-time costs you might also see

  • SEO audit: $500 to $5,000 depending on site size. RankEmpire's initial audit is free and delivered within two business days, because it becomes the roadmap if you go ahead.
  • Website migration or redesign support: $3,000 to $30,000, driven by URL count and complexity. Skipping this is the most common cause of traffic collapse after a redesign.
  • Content production: $150 to $1,500 per piece depending on depth and expertise, if bought separately from a retainer.
  • Link building campaigns: $200 to $1,000 per earned editorial link when priced individually. Be suspicious of anything cheaper.

FAQ

How much does SEO cost per month for a small business?

Most small businesses pay between $500 and $2,500 a month. Single-location local service businesses are at the lower end; businesses in competitive metros or with several locations are at the upper end. RankEmpire's local plans start at $599 a month; see SEO services for small business.

Is it cheaper to do SEO yourself?

In cash, yes. In time, usually not. A disciplined owner can handle the basics in about five hours a week using guides like our local SEO checklist and the free tools on this site. Agencies make sense when that time is worth more spent on the business, or when the market is competitive enough to need specialist work.

Why do agencies not publish prices?

Because scope varies, and because some prefer to anchor to your budget. We publish ours because a clear price is easier to compare and keeps the conversation about the work rather than the negotiation.

How long do I have to commit?

Avoid long lock-ins. A three-month onboarding period is reasonable because the early work is front-loaded; after that, month to month with 30 days notice is fair. That is how RankEmpire plans work.

Does a higher price mean better results?

Not by itself. Price reflects hours and overhead. What predicts results is the method, the people doing the work and the fit between the plan and your market. Use the seven questions above to compare, not the fee, and our longer guide on how to choose an SEO company for the full checklist and red flags.

What results should I expect for the money?

Early movement on branded and low-competition terms within four to eight weeks, meaningful ranking gains for core terms in four to six months, and lead growth that compounds from there. Any agency promising specific positions is guessing or cheating.

Why is SEO so expensive?

Good SEO is skilled labor spread over months: technical fixes, content written by people who know the subject, reviews and links that have to be earned, and reporting. Most of the fee pays for those hours. Cheap plans usually cut the parts that move rankings, such as content and earned links, which is why they often produce reports but few leads.

How much does an SEO audit cost?

A standalone SEO audit commonly costs from about $500 for a small site to $5,000 or more for a large site with complex technical issues. RankEmpire's SEO audit is free and delivered within two business days.

How much does local SEO cost?

Local SEO for a single-location business usually costs about $500 to $1,500 a month, depending on how competitive the city and the service are. Multi-location businesses pay more because each location needs its own profile and pages. RankEmpire's local SEO packages start at $599 a month.

How much should SEO cost for a small business?

Enough to pay for real work each month: technical fixes, content and earned links or local signals. For most small businesses that is $500 to $2,500 a month. Plans far below that range usually skip the work that moves rankings.
