Overview

Technical problems rarely look dramatic. A canonical tag pointing the wrong way, a slow template, a render-blocking script or an orphaned section can quietly hold back an entire site. We find these issues with full crawls and log data, then fix them at the source.

In depth

What is technical SEO?

Technical SEO is the work of making sure search engines can crawl, render, index and understand a website efficiently.

It covers site architecture, XML sitemaps, robots.txt, canonical tags, redirects, status codes, page speed, Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, structured data and JavaScript rendering. Technical SEO does not create new content. It removes the barriers that stop good content from being found and ranked. A site with excellent pages can still underperform if Google wastes time on duplicate URLs, hits redirect loops or cannot render key content.

Technical issues often appear after changes, such as a redesign, a platform migration, a new plugin or a developer deploying a staging robots.txt to production. They also build up slowly on large sites as filters, tags and old pages multiply. Technical SEO combines crawler tools like Screaming Frog with Google Search Console data and server logs to find these problems, then turns them into specific tickets your developers can implement. For e-commerce and SaaS sites with many templates, this work often deserves the first share of the budget.

Why it matters

Why technical SEO matters for your website

01

Content cannot rank if it is not indexed

If search engines cannot crawl or index a page, nothing else you do for that page matters. Technical SEO confirms that your important pages are discoverable, rendered correctly and included in the index where searchers can find them. Everything else in SEO depends on this base.

02

Better use of crawl resources

On large sites, search engines may spend their crawling on parameter URLs, thin tag pages or redirect chains. Cleaning these up directs attention to the pages that actually bring in customers and revenue for your business. New and updated pages also get picked up sooner as a result.

03

Faster, more usable pages

Page experience affects both users and search. Fixing slow loading, layout shifts and mobile problems helps visitors stay, read and convert, and removes a possible disadvantage against faster competitors in the same results. Faster templates also make every future page launch start from a stronger position.
